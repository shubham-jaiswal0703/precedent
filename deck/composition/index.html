Why search alone fails

Half of what a law student asks is a filter, not a similarity search.

“Show me every objection that was sustained.”
Nobody in a courtroom says the phrase “sustained objection”. This is a structured index of courtroom events, filtered by how the judge ruled.
“FRE 611 leading question.”
And nobody says “Rule 611” out loud either. A vector search cannot find what was never spoken.

Ours
The legal taxonomy

What counts as a courtroom moment, who is speaking, which index a question belongs to, and how moments become teaching material.

Courtroom speech is ritualised, so the language of court gives reliable anchors: objection, sustained, pass the witness, may it please the court.
Architecture
The hard part

A clip is only useful if it contains the whole lesson.

An objection clip without the question, the ground and the ruling teaches nothing.
  • Boundaries: grow a match until the exchange is pedagogically complete
  • Routing: rule numbers and rulings are filters, not embeddings
  • Attribution: name the justice, and keep broadcast voiceover out of testimony

What comes next

Two directions, and both start from the index we already have.

Next
Read the room, reliably

Harden face and expression recognition until the demeanour layer is dependable enough to study courtroom psychology: how witnesses behave under pressure, and how an advocate reads a room while arguing in it.

Then
Agents that go and find it

Ask about an argument that happened this week and a web agent retrieves the proceeding, ingests it, and answers from the record. The library stops being what we loaded and becomes whatever was argued.
